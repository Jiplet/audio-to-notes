# Interview notes prompt

Use this for: customer interviews, user research, discovery calls, expert interviews. Optimised for extracting themes and quotable lines.

---

You will receive an audio recording of an interview.

**Step 1: Transcribe**
Produce a verbatim transcript with speaker labels ("Interviewer" and "Interviewee", or names if I provide them). Add timestamps every 2 minutes.

**Step 2: Summarise**
After the transcript, produce a structured summary with these sections:

### One-line takeaway
The single most important thing the interviewee said or revealed. One sentence.

### Themes
Group the conversation into 3 to 6 themes. For each theme:
- **Theme name** (3 to 5 words)
- 2 to 4 bullets summarising what was said
- One direct quote that captures the theme best, with timestamp

### Quotable lines
Pull 5 to 10 standout direct quotes. Verbatim. Each with timestamp. Choose lines that are:
- Specific (not generic "we want better tools" type quotes)
- Surprising or counterintuitive
- Emotionally loaded
- Actionable as evidence in a write-up

### Contradictions and tensions
Places where the interviewee said two things that don't quite fit, or where their words and tone diverged. Bullet list. If none, write "None noted."

### Follow-up questions
Things I should have asked but didn't, or questions raised by what they said. Bullet list, prioritised top to bottom.

### Confidence flags
Where in the recording was the audio unclear, the interviewee uncertain, or the answer obviously rehearsed? Bullet list with timestamps. These are sections to treat carefully.

---

**Rules**
- Quotes must be verbatim. If unclear, mark as *"[unclear]"* rather than paraphrasing.
- Themes should emerge from the interview, not be imposed from outside.
- Do not editorialise. The interviewee's views, however unfamiliar, should be represented faithfully.
- Flag any place I led the witness or asked a leading question.
